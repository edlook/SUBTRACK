Annual vs monthly plans: when is the yearly plan actually cheaper?

By the SubTrack team · Last updated: November 4, 2026

Quick answer

A yearly plan is cheaper if you will use the service for more months than the break-even point, which is the yearly price divided by the monthly price. If a yearly plan costs 8.25 times the monthly price, you need at least 9 months of use to come out ahead. If you are not sure you will keep the service that long, monthly billing is the safer choice.

Yearly plans are often advertised as a discount, and they frequently are. But a discount only helps if you use what you pay for. The right choice depends on how many months you will really use the service, not on the label “best value”.

The break-even formula

Two numbers are enough: the monthly price (M) and the yearly price (Y).

Break-even months = Y ÷ M

This is the number of months of monthly billing that would cost the same as one year on the yearly plan. If you expect to use the service for longer than that, the yearly plan is cheaper. If you expect to use it for fewer months, monthly billing is cheaper, because you can stop paying at any time.

Two related figures are useful:

  • Effective monthly price of the yearly plan: Y ÷ 12.
  • Saving over a full year of use: (M × 12) − Y. If this is zero or negative, the yearly plan is not a discount at all.

A worked example (hypothetical numbers)

These prices are invented for illustration and are not any real service’s prices. Suppose a service charges $12 per month or $99 per year.

  • Break-even months = 99 ÷ 12 = 8.25, so you need 9 months of use for the yearly plan to be cheaper.
  • Effective monthly price of the yearly plan = 99 ÷ 12 = $8.25.
  • Saving over a full year of use = (12 × 12) − 99 = 144 − 99 = $45.

What the formula does not capture

  • Seasonal use. If you only need a tool for a project, an exam period or a sports season, you can subscribe monthly for those months and cancel in between.
  • Price changes. A monthly plan lets you react quickly if the price rises. A yearly plan locks in today’s price until renewal, which is good if prices rise and a drawback if the service gets worse.
  • Cash flow. A yearly plan means a larger payment at once. That is fine for some people and uncomfortable for others.
  • Different features. Occasionally the yearly and monthly plans are not identical. Compare what is included before you compare the price.
  • Your own track record. If you often forget to cancel, an annual plan bills you once and keeps you for a year even if you stop using it. A monthly plan limits the damage.

Refunds, cancellation and price changes

The cheapest-looking plan is not cheap if you cannot get out of it. Before you pick the yearly option, check three things in the service’s terms and billing page:

  1. Refund policy. Some services refund the unused part of a yearly plan, some refund only within a short window, and some do not refund at all. Rules also differ by country and by where you bought it (the service itself, or an app store).
  2. What cancelling does. Usually cancelling stops the next renewal but keeps access until the paid period ends. Confirm this, because it decides whether you can cancel right after buying and still use the full year.
  3. Renewal price. Introductory yearly prices sometimes rise at renewal. The price shown at sign-up is not always the price you will be charged next year.

Trials add another trap: some free trials convert straight into a yearly plan, so the first charge is the whole year rather than one month. Read the confirmation email, and see our free trial reminder guide for how to avoid that.

Never be surprised by a yearly renewal

A yearly plan has one weakness: you only see the charge once a year, so you forget it exists. The fix is to record the renewal date when you buy and set a reminder well ahead of it, for example two weeks before, so you have time to decide whether to keep, switch to monthly or cancel.

A simple way is to add the plan to a list that shows both monthly and yearly cost, so a $99 yearly plan appears as $8.25 a month next to your other subscriptions. A quick decision checklist

  • Calculate Y ÷ M and write down the break-even months.
  • Be honest: will you use it for longer than that? If you are unsure, choose monthly.
  • Read the refund and cancellation terms for the yearly plan.
  • Check whether the renewal price is the same as the price you see today.
  • Set a reminder two weeks before the renewal date, and another a few days before.
  • Once a year, repeat the calculation: your usage may have changed.
